When an AEC firm talks to us about AI, the first question is almost always "which tool should we use?" It is the wrong first question. The one that comes first is: **can we use it at all?**

AI governance, meaning the rules for what staff may use, with what information, and who decides, is as important as the AI you want to use. It comes first, not after the rollout.

## Shadow IT has never been more costly

Shadow IT is what happens when people use software the firm has not approved. It has always existed. A designer signs up for a file-sharing site because it is faster than the firm's own system. Someone installs a plugin that nobody reviewed.

AI makes it far more common, and the stakes are higher. The tools are free or cheap, take a minute to start, and are useful right away. Nobody thinks of pasting a paragraph into a chat window as installing software. But it is a decision about where your firm's information goes.

## Why the data matters

Consider what staff paste into AI tools in a normal week: project descriptions, client names, specifications, drawing notes, emails, and sometimes models and drawings.

Many AI services can use what you give them to improve their products, and the rules differ widely between vendors, between free and business plans, and from one setting to the next. Even where you can opt out, it is worth asking how you would know the opt-out is being honored. We would not assume that "do not train on my data" means your wording, your IP and your models never leave your control. Read the actual terms for the actual plan your staff are using. If you cannot answer the question, treat it as unanswered.

## Check before you push anything out

Before the firm rolls out, or even officially tolerates, any AI tool, check with your lawyers and look at what you have already signed:

- **Client contracts.** Do they say anything about AI, third-party tools or where project information may be processed?
- **NDAs.** Many were written before AI tools were common. Sharing confidential material with a third-party service may not be permitted, whatever the tool promises.
- **Government and regulated work.** Some projects come with rules about where data may go and who may handle it.
- **Your own IP.** Your models, details and standards are the asset. Decide what may never leave the firm.
- **Insurance.** Ask your broker whether AI use affects your coverage.

We are not lawyers, and this list is a starting point for that conversation, not legal advice.

## What a governance model looks like

It does not need to be long. A small firm can start with a single page:

1. **An approved list.** Which AI tools staff may use, and on which plan or account.
2. **What may go in.** What information is fine, what needs approval, and what is never allowed.
3. **Who decides.** One person who can say yes or no to a new tool quickly, so that people do not work around the rule.
4. **How it is checked.** Your IT team can see which AI services are being used across the firm and compare that with the approved list.
5. **A review date.** The tools change fast, so the rules need a regular look.

A rule that is too strict gets ignored, and then you have shadow IT again. The aim is a clear, quick path to yes for the tools that are safe to use.
