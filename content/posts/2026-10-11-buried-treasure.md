## Here's the dig, step by step.

### The map

A client's software renewal was coming in at $105 per user per month. We thought that was too high.

### Step 1: Check our permission slip.

As their MSP, we hold secondary admin on their accounts, and they've given us the authority to negotiate and make billing decisions. We use it carefully. The client always gets the final say.

### Step 2: Ask the client for their walk away price.

- "We need this tool, so there is no walk away price."
- Good to know. That told us we couldn't just threaten to leave, so we'd need a different play.

### Step 3: Wait for the right moment.

The renewal was 45 days out. We agreed to start the conversation at 30 days, once it was officially in the renewal window.

### Step 4: Make the vendor believe we can leave.

We told them we were looking at dropping to 5 users and moving to another product. But if they could get to $60 per user, we'd be happy to stay.

### Step 5: Know what the client actually uses.

The $60 tier was a different tier, and the client would lose some functionality. But we manage their environment, so we knew they weren't using those extras. Giving up features nobody touches costs nothing.

### Step 6: Let the vendor do the math.

They came back at $70 per user.

## X marks the spot: about $11,000 saved, for 20 minutes of phone calls.

Notice what actually did the work.

Not tough talk. Just:

- IT that knows the landscape
- Permission from the client
- Timing
- A credible alternative
- Knowing real usage and license tiers

Your firm has a map like this somewhere. Software renewals are just the easiest place to start digging. This works with Software, Hardware, Services, Consultants - there's treasure to be dug up everywhere if you look for it.
