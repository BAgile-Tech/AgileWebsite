<div class="prose">
  <p>Most onboarding problems aren't technical. They come from nobody writing down what the new person needs until the day they show up. The process below moves every decision earlier, and it leaves the office manager with a record of exactly what was delivered.</p>

  <h2>1. The setup sheet</h2>
  <p>It starts with one form the office manager fills in as soon as an offer is accepted. It asks for the basics we need, and nothing we can look up ourselves.</p>
  <div class="table-wrap"><table class="doc-table">
    <caption>Example setup sheet fields</caption>
    <thead><tr><th scope="col">Field</th><th scope="col">Why we ask</th></tr></thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td>Name and start date</td><td>Accounts and hardware ready the day before</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Role</td><td>Picks the role profile (step 2)</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Office, remote or hybrid</td><td>Desk setup on site, or hardware shipped to their home</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Manager and project teams</td><td>File and project platform access</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Anything unusual</td><td>Extra software, accessibility needs, a specific plugin</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table></div>

  <h2>2. The role profile, or avatar</h2>
  <p>Every firm we support has a small set of role profiles. We call them avatars. Each one defines the software, file access and class of hardware a person in that role needs. A new hire is matched to an avatar instead of starting from a blank list.</p>
  <div class="table-wrap"><table class="doc-table">
    <caption>Example avatars (each firm's set is different)</caption>
    <thead><tr><th scope="col">Avatar</th><th scope="col">Core software</th><th scope="col">Hardware class</th></tr></thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td>Designer</td><td>Revit, Rhino, Enscape, Bluebeam, Microsoft 365, Egnyte</td><td>Design workstation with a dedicated GPU</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Project manager</td><td>Revit (viewing), Bluebeam, ACC or Procore, Microsoft 365, Egnyte</td><td>Business laptop with a docking station</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Visualization</td><td>Enscape or other render tools, Rhino, Adobe Creative Cloud</td><td>High-end GPU workstation</td></tr>
      <tr><td>Operations and finance</td><td>Microsoft 365, accounting and HR platforms, Egnyte</td><td>Business laptop</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table></div>
  <p>Avatars make onboarding consistent, and they make the next steps faster because most of the answers are already decided.</p>

  <h2>3. The approved software list</h2>
  <p>The avatar pulls from the firm's approved software list. If it's not on the list, it doesn't get installed without a quick approval. That keeps licensing clean, keeps unvetted tools off firm machines, and it's the same list your AI policy points to when someone asks whether a new AI tool is allowed.</p>

  <h2>4. Software assets and licenses</h2>
  <p>Before anything is installed, we check the firm's license records. Is there a free Autodesk seat? A Bluebeam license from someone who left? If a new seat has to be bought, the office manager hears about the cost before it's ordered, not when the invoice arrives. The assignment is recorded against the person in the asset register.</p>

  <h2>5. Is the hardware fit for purpose?</h2>
  <p>Next we compare the machine that's available against the avatar's hardware class. A laptop that was fine for a project manager may struggle with Enscape for a designer. If an existing machine fits, it's redeployed. If it doesn't, the office manager gets a quote with the reason, in time to order.</p>

  <h2>6. Set a hardware review date</h2>
  <p>Every machine that goes out gets a review date in the asset record. When that date comes up, we check the machine against the role it's serving now. Refreshes get planned and budgeted instead of forced by a failure in the middle of a deadline.</p>

  <h2>7. The setup</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>Microsoft 365 and Entra ID account, with multi-factor authentication</li>
    <li>Egnyte access and the right project and department folders</li>
    <li>ACC, BIM 360 or Procore access for their projects</li>
    <li>Software from the avatar, including the Revit versions their projects need</li>
    <li>Endpoint protection, backup and device management</li>
    <li>Printers, plotters and email signature</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Then we test it the way the new hire will use it: sign in, open a project file, print.</p>

  <h2>8. A video walkthrough to the office manager</h2>
  <p>The last step is a short screen recording of the finished setup: accounts signed in, software installed, project files opening. It goes to the office manager, who can forward it to the new hire as a first-day orientation.</p>
